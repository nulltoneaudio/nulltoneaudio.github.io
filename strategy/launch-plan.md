# Getting NULLTONE off the ground

A 90-day plan. Not a mood board — a sequence, with numbers and deadlines.

---

## The honest starting point

Right now: a name, a site, and no released music. That's a normal place to start, but it changes what the next 90 days are for.

**You do not have a marketing problem yet. You have a catalogue problem.**

Nobody follows a DJ for the logo. They follow because a track hit them, or they were in the room when a set worked. Everything below is sequenced around getting two finished records and one mix into the world, then using those to get in front of rooms and promoters. The site, the handles and the visuals exist to make that credible — not to substitute for it.

**The one decision to make before day 1: your lane.** Pick one: bass/breaks, house, techno, or open format. Write it down. Every post, every track, every DM for 90 days points at that lane. You can move later, once people know what they're following. Switching mid-build is the single most common way this stalls.

---

## The three assets that get you booked

Almost every booking comes from someone hearing one of these three things. Nothing else in this plan matters if they don't exist.

1. **Two finished tracks.** Finished, not perfect. Mastered, cover art, released.
2. **One 30–45 minute mix.** Recorded in one take, no edits, with a tracklist. This is your calling card; it's what a promoter plays while deciding.
3. **One good photo set.** Three usable images: a portrait, a shot at the decks, one atmospheric frame. Phone is fine if the light is hard and the room is dark.

Everything else — the site, the bio, the grid — is packaging for these.

## Phase 1 · Days 1–30: Build the assets

**Goal: two tracks ready to release, one mix recorded, all accounts claimed.**

| Week | Focus | Output by Sunday |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Identity + setup | Handles claimed, site live, photos shot, bio and avatar identical everywhere |
| 2 | Track A | Arrangement finished, mixed on monitors, checked on a phone and a car |
| 3 | Track B + mix | Track B arranged, 30–45 min mix recorded in one take with a tracklist |
| 4 | Release A | Mastered, cover art, distributed, announced, first 9 posts live |

**Rules for this phase**

- Finish, don't polish. Two released average tracks beat one unreleased great one — the first release teaches you the whole pipeline (mastering, distribution, metadata, cover specs) while the stakes are low.
- Master cheaply: a $50–80 per-track engineer on Fiverr or a friend who knows what they're doing. Self-mastering is fine for the first one if it's not clipping.
- Distribution: DistroKid (~$23/yr, unlimited) or Amuse (free tier, slower). Pick one, upload, done.
- Do not spend money on ads, a logo redesign, or a plugin. Spend it on mastering and photos.

**Done looks like:** a private SoundCloud link with two finished tracks, a mix file, and a folder of three photos.

## Phase 2 · Days 31–60: Get in the room

**Goal: three real gigs, even unpaid ones, and footage from each.**

This phase is not online. It's physical, and it's uncomfortable.

**The scene loop, every week**

- **Go out twice.** Local nights in your lane. Not to network — to be a recognizable face. Be there early, stay late, talk to the person who booked it.
- **Meet three new people per week.** One promoter, one DJ, one person who just loves the music.
- **Send five DMs per week** to promoters, radio hosts and DJs you actually rate (template below).
- **Play one set anywhere.** House party, friend's bar, radio slot, warehouse. Free is fine; footage is the payment.

**What to say when you get the gig**

> Bring a plan, not a playlist. Know your first three tracks and your last one. Know what time you play and what the room needs then — an opener is not a headliner. Bring your own controller, a backup USB and a cable you know works.

**Capture everything**

One 15-second vertical clip per set, filmed by a friend, from behind the booth or in the crowd. This is the raw material for the entire next phase. No clip means the gig barely counts online.

**Pitch email — keep it this short**

```
Subject: NULLTONE — set for [NIGHT / DATE]

Hey [NAME],

I'm NULLTONE, a [GENRE] DJ/producer out of [CITY]. I've got a [LENGTH] set
built for a room like [VENUE] — here's a recent one: [LINK]

Two tracks out this year, both self-released: [LINK]

If you've got an opener slot or a support date coming up, I'd love to be
considered. Happy to send a full plan for the night.

— NULLTONE
nulltone@icloud.com
```

**Follow up once, seven days later, in one line.** Then stop. Silence is not rejection, it's a queue.

## Phase 3 · Days 61–90: Turn proof into money

**Goal: your first paid booking, and a repeatable content engine.**

- **Release Track B** on the same cadence as A — a single every 4–6 weeks keeps you in feeds without spamming.
- **Publish mix #2**, recorded live at one of the Phase 2 gigs. Real room, real crowd noise. That recording is more convincing than any studio mix.
- **Cut a 45-second showreel** from the clips: three rooms, one drop each, the mark at the end.
- **Build the press kit** (one PDF): 150-word bio, three photos, two links, one line on what you play, fee range.
- **Ask for money.** The ladder: free → $150 → $300 → $600. Move up when you've played the room once and it went well. Never apologize for the number; give a range and a reason.
- **Send ten booking emails** with the reel and the mix. Ten is the number where one reply is statistically normal.

**Day 90 scorecard — the only numbers that matter**

| Metric | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Released tracks | 2 |
| Mixes published | 2 |
| Live sets played | 3 |
| Promoter relationships | 5 |
| Instagram followers | 500–1,000 (vanity — ignore if the rest is on track) |
| SoundCloud plays | 1,000+ across tracks |
| Paid bookings | 1 |

If you hit the first four and miss the followers, you're fine. If you hit followers and miss the gigs, you built an audience with nothing to book.

## The social system

Three posts a week, forever. Not daily. Daily is how people quit in week three.

| Day | Pillar | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mon | **Process** | Studio detail, a version comparison, the desk at 2am. Low effort, high credibility |
| Wed | **Music** | A clip, a track, a mix excerpt. The thing you want to be known for |
| Sat | **The room** | Show footage, crowd, flyer, recap. Proof you actually play |

Stories daily, five minutes: one detail, one track you're into, one repost.

**Hook formulas for clips** (first two seconds decide everything)

1. "This is what [GENRE] sounds like at 3am."
2. "I made this kick out of [unexpected source]."
3. "Nobody was ready for this drop." *(only if the drop is genuinely good)*
4. "Here's the version I almost released."
5. "Playing this in [CITY] on [DATE] — first time anyone's heard it."

**What not to post**

- Gear photos with no music. Nobody follows a controller.
- Screenshots of your DAW with no audio.
- "New music coming soon" with no date. Announce when it's done.
- Anything you'd be embarrassed to have a promoter see.

**Caption structure that works**

Line 1: the specific fact (BPM, key, city, date).
Line 2: one honest detail about how it was made.
Line 3: the ask (link in bio, tickets, comment).

Skip the poetry. The music is the poetry.

## Platform priority

| Platform | Job | Time per week |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Instagram | Discovery and proof you play | 3 posts, 5 stories, 15 min of DMs/day |
| SoundCloud | Where the music actually lives; what promoters check | Upload, tracklist, no maintenance |
| TikTok | Reach for clips that already worked on IG | Repost the same verticals, nothing bespoke |
| YouTube | Long-form mixes and sets | One upload per mix, chaptered |
| Email | Bookings and the people who actually care | Reply within 24h, keep a list of every contact |

Do not maintain five platforms with original content. Make one thing, post it everywhere it fits.

## What success actually looks like at day 90

Not a viral moment. This:

- Two tracks you'd still play in a year.
- One mix a promoter can listen to and decide from.
- Three rooms that know your name.
- One person who books you again.
- A system you can run on ten hours a week without burning out.

From there, the next 90 days are the same loop with better inputs: bigger rooms, better records, higher fees.

## Running it — start date, gates, tracker

This plan is now dated. **Block one starts 18 November 2026 and ends 16 February 2027** (13 weeks, 90 days). Everything is sequenced from that Monday; if you start late, move the date rather than compressing the weeks.

**The three gates.** Do not start the next phase because the calendar says so — start it because the gate is closed.

| Phase | Weeks | Gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01 Build the assets | 1–4 | Two finished tracks, one 30–45 min mix, three photos |
| 02 Get in the room | 5–8 | Three sets played with footage from each, five promoter relationships |
| 03 Turn proof into money | 9–13 | One paid booking, a reusable press kit, a release cadence |

If a gate is still open at the end of its phase, repeat the phase. A week spent on a closed gate is wasted; a week spent on an open one is the whole job.

**The tracker: `plan/index.html`.** Open `https://nulltoneaudio.github.io/plan/` and work from the top. It holds the same 31 tasks in the same order, with per-phase progress, a "do next" strip, the weekly counters and the day-90 scorecard. State saves in your browser, so export a backup if you switch devices or clear site data.

**The weekly loop, ten hours:**

- Mon — process post, 30 min. Wed — music post, 30 min. Sat — the room, 30 min.
- 5 DMs a week, 20 min each including the reply.
- Two nights out, one set anywhere.
- Sunday, 5 min: the check-in below, counters updated in the tracker.

## The weekly check-in

Every Sunday, five minutes, answer these:

1. Did I finish something this week? (a track, a mix, a clip, an email)
2. Did I send five DMs?
3. Did I go out twice?
4. What's the next deadline I've told someone else about?

If the answer to 1 or 2 is no for two weeks running, the plan isn't the problem — the schedule is. Cut something else.
